# 03. Application Access Decisions

I assessed each application against its business justification, permission scope, access governance and impact on business continuity.

| Application | Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| DocuSharePro | Remove | An unverified app with no business owner, extensive permissions and a weekend consent spike suggests OAuth consent phishing, justifying immediate revocation despite potential business disruption. |
| ExportBot | Restrict | Nightly clinic reporting provides legitimate business value, justifying continued operation while addressing missing ownership, an unchanged secret and unrestricted access to all mailboxes. |
| PDF Tools Online | Remove | An unverified app with no business owner retains broad read/write file permissions despite 190 days of inactivity, creating unnecessary exposure with no demonstrated business value. |
| MailMerge Plus | Keep | Although sending mail on behalf of users carries impersonation risks, it is a verified, business-critical Marketing tool with nine consenting users and an accountable business owner. |
| SignFast | Keep | Verified publisher, accountable business owner and read/write permissions limited to user-selected files, balancing business needs with least-privilege access. |
| SchedulePro | Keep | Despite its 64 consenting users, read-only permissions are proportionate to its scheduling function, supported by a verified publisher, active usage and an identified business owner. |
| HRLink | Keep | A verified HR platform with access limited to basic profile information for sign-in, presenting minimal data exposure risk while supporting 118 employees. |
| TaskBoard | Keep | Its verified publisher, active usage, identified business owner and calendar read/write permissions that appear proportionate to its intended workflow justify retaining access. |

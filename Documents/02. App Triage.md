# 02. App Triage

I prioritised the applications based on suspicious activity, permission scope, potential impact and urgency.

## Priority Applications

| Priority | Application | Why I'd act |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | DocuSharePro | Suspected OAuth consent phishing: 112 approvals in four calendar days, including 83 over the weekend, combined with an unverified publisher, broad permissions, offline access and no business owner. |
| P2 | ExportBot | Independent access to all mailboxes, an unchanged secret since March 2025 and a departed owner create an organisation-wide access risk. |
| P3 | PDF Tools Online | Unverified publisher, broad file permissions across 17 users, no business owner and 190 days of inactivity indicate potentially unnecessary access. |

<BR>

## Why This Order?

P1 -  **DocuSharePro** shows signs of a potentially active threat.<br> 
P2 -  **ExportBot** presents significant potential exposure without evidence of compromise.<br>
P3 -  **PDF Tools Online** retains broad permissions despite prolonged inactivity, making it less urgent but still a security concern.<br>

<BR>

## ExportBot: The Hidden Risk

ExportBot could be more dangerous than DocuSharePro because its application permissions allow independent access to every mailbox. If its long-standing secret were compromised, an attacker could access those mailboxes without signing in as a user or completing MFA.

Its internal origin and nightly activity demonstrate a business purpose, but do not establish that its permissions are justified or its credentials secure.

<BR>

## Likely Legitimate Applications

| Application | Why it looks worrying | Why it is probably legitimate |
